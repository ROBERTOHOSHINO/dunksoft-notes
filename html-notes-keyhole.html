The Japanese tumulus form is called a keyhole-shaped tomb in English.

 

Today, while my authentication patent is pending, I stood on top of Japan’s oldest “keyhole” — Mori Shogunzuka Kofun, built 1,600 years ago.

 

No one knows the king’s name. Yet he is still remembered — through shape, stone, and place.

 

The monument is the public layer; the king’s identity, the private layer. Public-key architecture was standing on a mountain in ancient Shinano, sixteen centuries early.
